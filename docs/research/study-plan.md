# SID Song Crafter study plan

## Product and outcome

**SID Song Crafter — a composition and sound-design workbench for the Commodore 64 SID chip, supporting single- and dual-SID music.** Map given, attributed musical scores into expressive single- and dual-SID arrangements. Original score composition is outside the current remit; creativity belongs in the SID interpretation. Its value is making the **next great-sounding song easier to create** through a growing, executable repertoire. The playback engine is one component alongside source preservation, SID-native authoring, program analysis, musical knowledge and listening comparisons.

Each study must leave reusable instruments, effects or arrangement methods, with evidence connecting **musical intention → editable description → SID programming** and back. Follow the [analysis procedure](tools/sid-program-analysis.md), [composition workflow](../workflows/decoded-song-workflow.md) and [technique index](technique-index.md). This plan owns priorities and completion state; owning studies retain findings and provenance.

## Completion contract for every study

1. **Identify and map.** Pin archive URL, bytes/hash, subtune, credits, chip/clock, cadence and complete musical form. Check interviews, manuals and original/commented sources alongside the binary; identify the composer's editor and driver where evidence permits.
2. **Explain mechanisms.** Connect executed instructions, state and ordered SID writes to instruments, effects and musical roles. Preserve initialization, release evolution, shared filters, oscillator dependencies and meaningful within-frame timing. Use a targeted intervention to test a causal explanation.
3. **Reproduce independently.** Generate the sound through our own code from editable musical/effect descriptions. Original driver reuse and captured-register replay do not satisfy this stage. Retain the original as a comparison oracle.
4. **Verify and listen.** Prove the supported execution contract, then compare the characteristic phrase in context and the complete selected form, including its ending. Record residual timing/model differences and listening feedback separately. A pleasing excerpt cannot close the complete-song task.
5. **Transfer and retain.** Apply each promoted mechanism to different musical material, preferably a familiar source-backed phrase, with named parameters, constraints and an executable example. Supply a same-material reference for the familiar transfer and listen for musical usefulness and a suitable ending; a transposed note or matching register trace alone is insufficient. Preserve the explicitly accepted Flashdance original-composition transfer under the [new-material exception](../workflows/audio-comparisons.md#package-a-listening-review): its standalone authored phrase has no original recording or plain same-material comparison, and does not establish a controlled cross-palette judgment. Record the reuse outcome, update the owning library card and index, and retain useful findings, not work diaries.

Mark a primary deliverable **done** only when all five stages have linked evidence and its declared musical deliverable has been reviewed. For M1–M6, the whole composer study additionally requires the [contrasting-reference check](#required-contrasting-references-for-the-six-composers), with verified findings and updated reuse constraints; a completed primary alone cannot close that study. Track stages as `queued`, `active`, `awaiting listening`, or `done`; a new artifact or rendered WAV alone does not advance the verdict.

For active and future musical cases, account for the [model-assistance review checkpoints](audio-assessment.md#use-and-reassess-model-assistance) before closure. A recorded decision not to use a model can satisfy a review; unassessed usefulness cannot. This does not reopen already accepted studies solely to add model calls.
