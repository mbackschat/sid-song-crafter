# FORM-01 — recognize the theme, vary its return

**Status:** original [Für Elise implementation](../../../../songs/studies/fuer-elise-interpretation/README.md) rendered; listening approval pending. The variations below remain design options unless implemented in that study. Browse the [gesture library](README.md).

## Musical purpose

Preserve a theme's identifiable contour and harmonic arrivals while giving its repeat a new character. For Für Elise, keep the opening E/D-sharp figure recognizable, then use accompaniment rhythm, bass register, chord answers and selected articulations to create a more playful return. Record added ornaments or changed melody notes explicitly; source preservation belongs to the retained original, not to a promise that every creative version is literal.

## A bounded variation recipe

1. Establish a clear first statement with space around the melody.
2. On the return, change one structural feature: add a bass answer, shift a chord inversion or replace a held accompaniment with a short burst.
3. Give a selected longer melody note pulse development or delayed vibrato; short notes may end before those programs develop.
4. Leave a deliberate gap or reduce texture at the cadence so the phrase has an ending.

Keep these as editable decisions in a new arrangement. Avoid random timing changes presented as expressiveness; each displacement should serve an accent, pickup, response or release. Improvisational freedom can be composed deliberately and reproduced exactly.

## Native dependencies and evaluation

The [moving pulse patch](../instruments/lead-02-moving-pulse.md) offers continuous development using one unfiltered voice. [Bass answers](bass-answer.md) and [chord bursts](chord-bursts.md) consume accompaniment time and may compete with melodic detail. Choose their placement together rather than auditioning only solo patches.

Compare complete corresponding phrases, including their ending, at declared tempos and chip models. Ask whether the theme remains recognizable, the return adds character, and the density still leaves a foreground. The user's preference for DRAX's richness and playfulness motivates this exercise; it is not evidence about how DRAX composed or improvised. Retain promising and rejected variants with the musical reason, not a chronological work log.
