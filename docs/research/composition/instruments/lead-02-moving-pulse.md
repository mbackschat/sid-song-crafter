# LEAD-02 — moving pulse lead

Design and measurement date: 2026-10-12. Evidence level: **rendered experiment**; listening review pending. This original lead aims for sustained development without adding another voice. Intended test register: A3–C5 in scientific pitch notation. It implements the [LEAD-02 brief](../sound-design-repertoire.md) and pairs with the [bright pluck](pluck-01-bright-attack.md) and [shared bass](bass-02-kick-to-bass.md).

The editable [moving instrument](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/native/instruments/pulse-moving.swi), [static reference](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/native/instruments/pulse-static.swi), [candidate phrase](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/native/pulse-candidate.swm) and [reference phrase](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/native/pulse-reference.swm) come from the [original source](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/repertoire.json). No upstream example patch or melody was copied. Original project material has no separate license grant; Hermit's player/exporter notice applies to the upstream code, as recorded in [the fixture README](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/README.md).

Target: SID-Wizard 1.97 Extra, one MOS8580 at `$D400`, PAL 985248 Hz, one update per 19656-cycle video frame, approximately 50.1245 Hz. The instrument uses one oscillator, has no ring/sync dependency and does not enter or control the shared filter. Both variants use control `$1A`, hard-restart ADSR `$0F00`, playing ADSR `$08A5`, initial waveform `$09`, vibrato `$24` and delay 12. The waveform program selects gated pulse `$41` at the played pitch and then ends, leaving the player free to perform the delayed vibrato.

The static reference holds width `$600`. The candidate resets to `$400`, adds `$20` for 32 sweep executions, subtracts `$20` for 32 executions and loops to the positive sweep. The range `$400`–`$800` is centred on the static reference. Width resets at each explicit instrument selection; tied `$3F` notes preserve the existing envelope and program state. No keyboard tracking, detune or gate-off table override is used. The reference and candidate use identical ADSR, vibrato, note data and timing; only the pulse program differs. The initial width differs because it is part of the defined sweep trajectory, so the comparison tests the complete motion program rather than only its steady-state slope.

The phrase includes two-row repeated E4 notes, an E4 hold, a rest/re-entry, tied A4 and E4 notes, a change to the bright pluck, then a C5 return. The simultaneous mix pair also changes the pluck attack and bass transient; it does not isolate pulse motion. The [exact candidate write log](../../../../build/instrument-trials/pulse-candidate/pulse-candidate.writes.json) confirms pulse width 1024–2048 while gated; the reference remains at 1536. During the held E4, the restart frame is tick 98, pulse begins at tick 99 and pitch settles to word 5614 at tick 100. That word remains stable through tick 111, then vibrato first changes it at tick 112 and cycles between 5578 and 5650. This claim is bounded to Extra, 1x PAL and this program: its waveform table ends after establishing the pitch, so it does not repeatedly overwrite the continuous vibrato with arpeggio pitch updates. The complete reference and candidate frequency and ADSR trajectories match. Gate stays set through the tied-note changes at ticks 267 and 291; the final gate clear occurs at tick 362. Pulse/vibrato writes continue after release, so the quiet ending is verified in PCM separately.

The [trial report](../../../../build/instrument-trials/trial.json) records 501 RAM-only py65 calls at 50.124573 Hz, init cost 1944 cycles and peak play cost 1271 of 19656 cycles for both variants. The candidate player/music payload uses 3109 bytes at `$1000`; instrument names are cropped only in playback exports and remain in SWM/SWI. Both libsidplayfp 3.1.1/SIDLite MOS8580 renders contain 441000 samples at 44100 Hz, peak magnitude 4550, zero clipping and final-half-second AC RMS zero. Model comparison and hardware behaviour remain untested; the RAM-only audit does not measure interrupt/badline contention.

Reproduction uses the [generation and trial commands](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/README.md). Listen to [the pulse A/B](../../../../build/instrument-trials/comparisons/pulse.ab.wav) and [the full mix A/B](../../../../build/instrument-trials/comparisons/mix.ab.wav); original-amplitude [reference](../../../../build/instrument-trials/pulse-reference/pulse-reference.wav) and [candidate](../../../../build/instrument-trials/pulse-candidate/pulse-candidate.wav) renders are retained. The [pulse matching report](../../../../build/instrument-trials/comparisons/pulse.comparison.json) applies gain 0.991077 (-0.077853 dB) to A and unity to B using full-file AC RMS, including rests/tails. Listener, playback setup, date, verdict, rejected variants and specific reasons are unrecorded. Acceptance asks whether the movement remains useful across the tested range without weakening low notes or tiring the ear on the hold; these measurements make no sound-quality judgment.
