# BASS-02 — kick-to-bass shared voice

Design and measurement date: 2026-10-12. Evidence level: **rendered experiment**; the tested transient produced little or no perceived difference for the listener. This original shared-voice program gives a bass arrival a short falling-pitch triangle transient before the pulse body. Intended test register: A1–F2 in scientific pitch notation. It addresses the [BASS-02 brief](../sound-design-repertoire.md); the separate [kick](drum-01-falling-kick.md) demonstrates an independent percussion alternative.

The editable [kick-to-bass instrument](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/native/instruments/bass-kick.swi), [plain bass reference](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/native/instruments/bass-plain.swi), [candidate phrase](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/native/bass-candidate.swm) and [reference phrase](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/native/bass-reference.swm) originate in the [project source](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/repertoire.json). No example patch or third-party melody was copied. Original project material is covered by the [MIT license](../../../../LICENSE); [the fixture README](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/README.md) records the upstream player notice separately.

Target: SID-Wizard 1.97 Extra, one MOS8580 at `$D400`, PAL 985248 Hz, one update per 19656-cycle frame, approximately 50.1245 Hz. Both variants use one voice on channel 3, without ring/sync dependencies. Control is `$0A`, hard-restart ADSR `$0F00`, playing ADSR `$06B3`, initial waveform `$09`, no vibrato/detune and fixed pulse width `$600`. Each instrument selection resets the pulse and filter programs. Gate-off uses the ordinary release nibble 3; no gate-off table override is present.

The reference selects pulse `$41` at the played pitch immediately when the waveform table starts. The candidate inserts three gated-triangle `$11` rows at +24, +12 and 0 semitones, then selects the same pulse `$41` at 0. The [candidate write log](../../../../build/instrument-trials/bass-candidate/bass-candidate.writes.json) confirms restart at tick 2, triangle frequency words 3746, 1873 and 936 at ticks 3–5, then pulse `$41` at frequency 936 at tick 6; the body settles to 937 at tick 7. The reference already uses pulse and frequency 936 at tick 3, then 937 at tick 4. The triangle transient therefore occupies three PAL updates, approximately 59.85 ms. ADSR, width, filter program, note data and articulation match the reference. The candidate delays the pulse body's arrival by design, so a beat may gain impact while losing an immediate harmonic bass cue.

The bass is the only filtered voice in both mixes. It owns the shared low-pass filter with resonance 4, initial cutoff high byte `$80` and low bits 0, then 24 sweep executions at signed fine-cutoff step `$F0` (-16), nominally moving 1024 to 640. The lead and pluck are unfiltered and their oscillator writes remain intact. A second filtered instrument on a higher-priority channel would change ownership and invalidate this sound's mix assumptions. MOS6581 cutoff response requires separate calibration; the numeric cutoff is not a portable acoustic frequency.

The exact filter trajectories match across the complete A/B pair. During the held F2 the cutoff resets to 1024 at tick 99, falls in steps of 16 and reaches 640 at tick 123. `$D417=$44` and `$D418=$1F` confirm resonance 4, channel-3 routing and low-pass mode. Changing to the independent kick clears only the channel-3 routing bit at tick 314 (`$D417=$40`), then returning to bass restores it at tick 326 (`$44`); the pulse body resumes at tick 330 after the returning bass transient. Pulse width remains 1536 during bass operation. Both final gates clear at tick 362. This covers repeated arrivals, the held F2, rest/re-entry, the kick change and bass recovery without stopping companion oscillators.

The [trial report](../../../../build/instrument-trials/trial.json) records 501 RAM-only py65 calls, init cost 1944 cycles and peak play cost 1269 of 19656 cycles for both variants. The candidate player/music payload uses 3127 bytes at `$1000`. Both libsidplayfp 3.1.1/SIDLite MOS8580 renders contain 441000 samples at 44100 Hz, zero clipping and final-half-second AC RMS zero; peak magnitudes are 5189 for the reference and 4546 for the candidate. Model comparison and hardware behaviour remain untested; the CPU audit excludes interrupt/badline contention.

Reproduction uses the [generation and trial commands](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/README.md). Listen to [the bass A/B](../../../../build/instrument-trials/comparisons/bass.ab.wav) and [the full mix A/B](../../../../build/instrument-trials/comparisons/mix.ab.wav); original-amplitude [reference](../../../../build/instrument-trials/bass-reference/bass-reference.wav) and [candidate](../../../../build/instrument-trials/bass-candidate/bass-candidate.wav) renders are retained. The [matching report](../../../../build/instrument-trials/comparisons/bass.comparison.json) applies gain 0.965113 (-0.308435 dB) to A and unity to B using full-file AC RMS, including rests/tails. The [listening findings](../gestures/README.md) record little or no perceived difference in this pair; playback equipment and mix-level benefit remain unestablished. Acceptance asks whether bass harmony remains intelligible at every arrival while the transient adds useful rhythmic energy, particularly in the densest passage. The single-voice mechanism cannot produce an independent sustained bass and independent kick at the same moment.
