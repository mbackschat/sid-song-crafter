# DRUM-01 — falling triangle kick

Design and measurement date: 2026-10-12. Evidence level: **rendered experiment**; listening review pending. This original kick uses a fixed descending triangle program, without noise or combined waveforms. Its role is a short low percussion accent; the triggering note does not transpose the programmed pitch stages. It follows the [DRUM-01 brief](../sound-design-repertoire.md).

The [native instrument](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/native/instruments/kick-fall.swi) and [diagnostic phrase](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/native/kick.swm) are generated from the [original source](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/repertoire.json). The [bass phrases](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/README.md) also change briefly to this kick before returning to their bass patch. No upstream example patch or melody was copied. Original project material has no separate license grant; the [fixture README](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/README.md) retains the upstream player attribution separately.

Target: SID-Wizard 1.97 Extra, one MOS8580 at `$D400`, PAL 985248 Hz, one update per 19656-cycle frame, approximately 50.1245 Hz. One independent voice is used; ring/sync, vibrato, detune, pulse programming and filter routing are disabled. Control is `$0A`, hard-restart ADSR `$0F00`, playing ADSR `$0600` and initial waveform `$09`. The short no-sustain envelope and a programmed gate clear bound the tail.

The waveform table selects gated triangle `$11` at absolute G3, C3, G2 and C2, then command `$02` retains the preceding waveform while setting C1 and delaying the next row. The final row selects triangle with gate clear `$10` and pitch/detune NOPs. Native absolute-pitch bytes are `$AC`, `$A5`, `$A0`, `$99` and `$8D`; these correspond to scientific pitches rather than SID-Wizard's display octave labels. The [write log](../../../../build/instrument-trials/kick/kick.writes.json) confirms restart `$09` at tick 2, gated-triangle frequency words 3337, 2227, 1668 and 1113 at ticks 3–6, then 556 at ticks 7–9. Gate clears at tick 10, where the driver's frequency word becomes 557. Thus command `$02` holds the low stage for three updates, and the gated tonal program lasts seven updates, approximately 139.65 ms. There is no noise component to judge or noise oscillator reset dependency.

The solo phrase moves from sparse eight-row hits to denser four-row hits, contains a rest and ends with an explicit gate-off row. There is no controlled A/B reference for this diagnostic. The shared bass pair uses the same independent kick at its instrument-change point, so that point is controlled across both versions. The last hit begins at tick 339 and its own program clears the gate at tick 346, before the final explicit pattern gate-off. Filter registers remain un-routed throughout the kick diagnostic. The [trial report](../../../../build/instrument-trials/trial.json) records 501 RAM-only py65 calls, init cost 1944 cycles, peak play cost 1269 of 19656 cycles and a 3083-byte player/music payload at `$1000`. The libsidplayfp 3.1.1/SIDLite MOS8580 render contains 441000 samples at 44100 Hz, peak magnitude 4236, zero clipping and final-half-second AC RMS zero. Model comparison and hardware behaviour remain untested; the CPU audit excludes interrupt/badline contention.

Reproduction uses the [generation and trial commands](../../../../songs/studies/sidwizard-repertoire/README.md). The [kick WAV](../../../../build/instrument-trials/kick/kick.wav) retains original amplitude with unity gain and full-file AC RMS 369.053; the same program also appears at the instrument-change point in [the bass A/B](../../../../build/instrument-trials/comparisons/bass.ab.wav) and [mix A/B](../../../../build/instrument-trials/comparisons/mix.ab.wav). Listener, setup, date, verdict and rejected variants are unrecorded. Acceptance asks whether the fall produces a useful attack and low body with a short enough tail to avoid masking the following bass arrival. A correct descending trace establishes synthesis control, not a convincing drum sound.
