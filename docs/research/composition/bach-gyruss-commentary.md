## Musical criteria for our opening audition

These are our proposed criteria, inferred from the written reception and the trial's existing organ brief; they are not reports of hearing Gyruss or our candidate.

- **A decisive entrance:** the octave gestures should have audible authority and remain clear through rapid notes. The opening should establish character before the listener has to recognize the tune by pitch alone.
- **Harmonic body with three voices:** preserve the pedal foundation and the important upper/inner relationships through the sustained diminished passage. Document omitted notes. Judge whether the held texture has weight; audition any chord cycling specifically for whether it weakens that impression.
- **Energy with dramatic pacing:** runs should move purposefully, while the written pauses and fermatas create tension. Our organ-inspired treatment should retain those gestures even where a game-oriented adaptation values continuous motion.
- **Register and balance:** high notes should remain intelligible without dominating the pedal or making the sustained texture tiring. Envelope levels, static pulse width and any filter choice are musical candidates to evaluate, not settings inferred from these reviews.

Provide the matching organ-performance passage beside the raw single-SID render and ask the listener about entrance, sustained weight, harmonic clarity and pace. A future authorized Gyruss audio comparison would need the selected platform and passage named separately. Written expectations and actual musical acceptance remain different evidence.

### Differences that matter musically

| Dimension | Gyruss #1 | Our accepted Toccata | Likely musical consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening identity | Octave-doubled A–G–A ornament, followed by the descending gesture; a separate rapidly repeating pulse-pitch sweep occupies the third voice for roughly six seconds. | Opening ornament omitted; three register statements with an organ-oriented foundation. | Gyruss makes the entrance recognizably electronic and uses ornament and an independent effect for immediate activity; the listener specifically prefers this effect-rich beginning. |
| Pulse and forward motion | After roughly 12.3 seconds, D/A bass pitches change every ten NTSC calls, with a restart at the five-call midpoint: a D–D–A–A articulation supports a recurring Bach motif. | Phrase-dependent timing follows the larger Toccata, with deliberately compressed holds and gaps. | A recurring accompaniment gives Gyruss a steadier sense of propulsion. “Faster” alone misses this distinction: the initial gesture/cadence occupies about 12 seconds in both. |
| Form | Selects, rearranges and repeats material; the running D–E–F–D–E–F–G–E motif arrives around 15 seconds. | The corresponding motif arrives around 31.1 seconds after more intervening source material; the complete declared Toccata continues to its cadence. | Gyruss reaches and reinforces a catchy passage sooner; ours preserves more of the composition's changing argument. These are different forms, not equal-duration covers. |
| Voice roles and contrast | Third voice changes from electronic sweep to pitched support; the later bass figure supplies a motor beneath the foreground. | More persistent lead, pedal/bass and harmony roles; selective chord cycling fills dense harmony. | Gyruss changes the function of its limited voices more conspicuously. Ours emphasizes harmonic body and continuity. |
| Timbre strategy | Pulse-based tonal passage; the later musical mode decrements pulse width by nine each NTSC update on all three voices. A score event resets the width high byte while retaining its low byte. No active filter, oscillator sync or ring modulation in the bounded execution. | Pulse lead with a relatively slow native width cycle, filtered pulse bass and triangle+pulse harmony; no designed opening sweep. | Gyruss's character need not come from a more elaborate SID feature set. The rates, articulation and placement of simple sounds matter more than adding another effect category. |

The comparison uses Gyruss's declared **NTSC/6581** and our accepted **PAL/8580** settings. It is neither loudness-matched nor a controlled chip-model experiment. Those differences may contribute to timbre, but they do not explain away the observed rhythmic, formal and voice-allocation choices. Do not attribute “modern” to NTSC or to a supposedly superior SID model.

### Reuse without changing the accepted baseline

The goal is **intention → musical role → SID mechanism → tradeoff**, not a ranking of the two interpretations. Our version and Gyruss illustrate different successful aims. Select the desired musical function first; then choose the mechanism and voice budget that express it. A modern/game intention does not require abandoning the recognisable source, and fidelity does not require static chip sounds.

| Intention | Musical role | Concrete SID/arrangement choice supported here | Cost or limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Preserve Bach’s rhetoric with organ weight | Foreground gesture, pedal foundation, inner harmony and complete cadence | Our source-backed full form, stable bass/harmony roles, filtered pulse pedal, triangle+pulse harmony and selective chord cycling | Three voices require documented omissions; sustained weight alone does not supply a recurring groove. |
| Establish an electronic/game identity immediately | An effect alongside a recognisable melodic entrance | Gyruss keeps the octave-doubled Bach gesture in two voices and assigns the third a rapid repeating pulse-frequency sweep | That voice cannot simultaneously supply an independent harmonic part. A long or over-loud sweep could obscure the gesture; its placement and balance matter. |
| Make a recognisable entrance feel more active | Ornament the foreground rather than add a fourth part | Gyruss’s explicit A–G–A gesture; our accepted opening omits the printed prall | Ornament duration takes time from the note or changes phrase timing. Its source relationship must remain explicit. |
| Sustain game-like propulsion | A recurring lower pattern under a memorable motif | Gyruss’s regular bass subdivisions, earlier arrival of the running motif and phrase repetition | Changes the source form and accompaniment; constant motion can reduce dramatic breathing if applied everywhere. |
| Animate a tonal texture | Ongoing spectral motion within pitched voices | Gyruss’s later all-voice pulse-width updates; our slower lead-only pulse cycle gives a different distribution and amount of motion | Width affects harmonic balance differently by pitch; reset phase and short note duration determine how much of the motion develops. “More PWM” is not an adequate recipe. |
| Mark a transition or change of musical function | Reassign voices when a section ends | Gyruss’s executed internal mode changes, including the later move into a noise-based program | The final program’s exact in-game function is unverified. Reuse the role-change principle; do not infer that sustained noise is a musical ending to imitate. |

Keep this mapping as a selection aid, not a claim that these mechanisms will succeed in every context.
