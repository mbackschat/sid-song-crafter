# Chris Hülsbeck and Jeroen Tel: the instrument is a program

Research checked on 2026-10-12. These two composers make a strong case for putting composition tools and musical judgement at the centre of SID work. Their documented workflows join note writing, instrument programming, and playback-driver design. This note distinguishes first-hand recollections, archival commentary, inspected source, and proposed experiments. The referenced SID recordings have not been listened to or traced in this research pass; the listening questions below are an explicit next step.

## Chris Hülsbeck: synthesis made editable

The contemporary [64'er Soundmonitor article, October 1986](https://www.64er-magazin.de/8610/soundmonitor.html) describes how the competition entry *Shades* led to an editor around Hülsbeck's Musicmaster routine. Before that editor, he entered data through a machine-language monitor. The published manual separates track order, note data, sound definitions, and arpeggios; it also describes keyboard playing/recording and copying/transposing reusable material. This is a composing environment around a driver, rather than a list of POKEs.

Its instrument definitions already expose pulse-width envelopes, portamento, vibrato depth/speed/delay, fine detuning, and filter envelopes. Its eight-step arpeggios transpose the note that triggers them. Filter definitions include a choice of triggering voice, acknowledging that a shared filter needs ownership. These are concrete controls for the evolution of a sound after its onset. The article contains a packed hexadecimal program listing, not a conveniently rebuildable modern source tree. Treat its prose as historical documentation; validate register bits against the [SID specification](../../../skills/c64-sid/references/sid-chip-spec.md), since its high-pass bit description is inconsistent with the chip's MODE/VOL table. [Soundmonitor manual](https://www.64er-magazin.de/8610/soundmonitor.html)

Hülsbeck's [2011 Retrogaming Planet interview](https://www.retrogamingplanet.it/dal-c64-alliphone-chris-huelsbeck-ed-i-suoi-30-anni-di-musica/) describes two C64s: one for programming source, one for music creation/playback. He used Soundmonitor and a later TFMX editor with readable note names alongside hexadecimal data, previewed melodies on an Ensoniq ESQ-1, and built a hardware sampler with a friend for drums, bass, and effects. He explicitly links the Amiga TFMX sound-script concept to its C64 predecessor: a short drum before a bass could suggest two simultaneous roles. The Amiga's later seven-channel mixing is a separate platform technique, not seven SID oscillators.

For *The Great Giana Sisters*, the same interview attributes the in-game reggae influence to the Rainbow Arts boss and distinguishes the opening title's Madonna *True Blue* homage from the menu music. This is useful style evidence: rhythmic placement and a recognizable melody were compositional decisions before any register programming. It does not justify identifying an unnamed subtune by ear or treating every familiar arrangement as the original C64 version. [Composer's account](https://www.retrogamingplanet.it/dal-c64-alliphone-chris-huelsbeck-ed-i-suoi-30-anni-di-musica/)

In the [2025 Amazona interview](https://www.amazona.de/interview-chris-huelsbeck-der-soundmagier-teil-1/), he again explains Soundmonitor as a response to cumbersome hexadecimal entry. His discussion of TFMX sound macros makes the abstraction clear: triggering a note could start a small program that changes pitch or plays a sample. The interview's macro examples are in its Amiga discussion; they establish the composer's design habit, not the exact C64 command encoding. His later use of Furnace concerns a Mega Drive project, so it is not evidence that Furnace exported these historical C64 tunes.

An especially practical correction appears in [HVSC STIL v85](https://hvsc.c64.org/download/C64Music/DOCUMENTS/STIL.txt): Hülsbeck explains that his original *Shades* development machine had an unusual filter, making the widely played version more filtered than intended. The archive identifies `Shades_filter_corrected.sid` as closer to his intention. A beloved reference recording is therefore not an unquestionable timbre target: retain the exact file variant, chip model, and rendering settings before comparing filters.

My working interpretation is a melody-first, synthesizer-oriented workflow whose editor makes repeated musical and timbral experiments affordable. That interpretation follows the described process; it is not an executed-program study of *Shades*. For our Beethoven arrangement, the useful transfer is controlled timbral movement around preserved musical phrases, not importing Hülsbeck's samples or changing Beethoven's harmony.

## Jeroen Tel: arrangement and driver collaboration

Tel's [Domination interview](https://www.atlantis-prophecy.org/recollection/?id_interview=89&load=interviews) describes starting with a BASIC driver, then composing in Soundmonitor. At a computer meeting he met Charles Deenen, who could write a driver matching his needs; together they founded Maniacs of Noise. Tel describes their initial division as his music and Deenen's effects. This makes the Soundmonitor-to-custom-driver progression explicit and prevents crediting the entire MoN sound to one composer working alone.

In [Hugi #38](https://hugi.scene.org/online/hugi38/hugi%2038%20-%20demoscene%20interviews%20magic%20jeroen%20tel.htm), Tel says he studied Hubbard's arrangements, techniques, melodies, and sounds and initially modified Hubbard's routine. He contrasts Hubbard's instrumental imitation with Galway's synthesizer atmosphere, names *Supremacy* for its melody and arrangement, and describes ideas coming from piano and experimenting with drums, basslines, chords, and melody in Renoise. These are distinct levels of work: a good driver makes an idea executable; it does not select the idea.

The [2010 PlayStation interview, part 1](https://blog.de.playstation.com/2010/07/05/computer-geschichte-interview-mit-jeroen-tel-1-teil/) identifies pop, electronic music, Hubbard, and a broad family listening background among his influences. He describes his preferred result as melodic, harmonic, funky, danceable music that sustains repeated listening. That supports studying groove, phrase variation, and register separation alongside impressive sound effects.

[Part 2](https://blog.de.playstation.com/?p=4937) provides an unusually concrete arrangement account. Rapid chord arpeggios free two voices for melody and bass; short percussion sounds interrupt and precede bass notes, allowing bass and drums to share a physical voice. He describes entering notes, rests, and sounds in assembler and retaining a preference for that method. He also identifies sampled drums and his own voice in *Turbo Outrun*. The translated interview calls SID voices and percussion “FM”; the [chip specification](../../../skills/c64-sid/references/sid-chip-spec.md) describes waveform oscillators, ring modulation, sync, envelopes, and filtering. Do not turn the interview's loose terminology into an unsupported hardware claim.

There is inspectable implementation evidence. The [recovered Cybernoid II source](https://github.com/realdmx/c64_6581_sid_players/blob/f215174fc4c983674808fcd47bc506f96b3d2d04/Tel_Jeroen_MON/Tel_Jeroen_Cybernoid2.asm), at revision `f215174fc4c983674808fcd47bc506f96b3d2d04`, credits Tel's music and Deenen's `MUSICFILE V01-07-1988` player. Its sound records contain waveform, attack/decay, sustain/release, filter selection, and three effects bytes. The inspected routines include pitch arpeggio, delayed vibrato, glides, pulse tables/arpeggio, waveform changes, and filter tables. It also contains separate bass, bass-drum, snare, and melodic sound definitions. This establishes implemented mechanisms; only an executed trace can identify their exact use at a particular musical moment.

The companion [MoN SFX player source](https://github.com/realdmx/c64_6581_sid_players/blob/f215174fc4c983674808fcd47bc506f96b3d2d04/Deenen_Charles_MON/Deenen_Charles_SFX_Player.asm) identifies conversion from TurboAssembler and retains a 1989 Scoop copyright notice. The [repository maintainer](https://github.com/realdmx/c64_6581_sid_players) explicitly describes a mix of original and reverse-engineered sources, converted for ACME, and warns that some listings need cleanup to build. Its [MIT license](https://github.com/realdmx/c64_6581_sid_players/blob/f215174fc4c983674808fcd47bc506f96b3d2d04/LICENSE) is the maintainer's stated software license; historical game/tune credits remain separate evidence. No archived tune or historical sound-data table is copied into our arrangement.

## Experiments for an expressive reusable instrument system

These are proposed original patches and tests. Start with isolated notes and short Beethoven phrases, then compare full sections at equal listening level. Use the [executed-program procedure](../tools/sid-program-analysis.md) to connect waveform, frequency, envelope and filter writes to the responsible code and instrument data. Controlled changes test an explanation; listening chooses the musical result.

| Experiment | Small controlled change | Proof criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delayed melodic expression | Hold long notes steady first; add shallow vibrato after an adjustable delay. Leave fast grace/32nd notes unmodulated. | Onset pitches and phrase rhythm remain identical; traces show no vibrato before the delay; listener prefers the sustained phrase without losing tonal centre. |
| Moving pulse instrument | Compare triangle, fixed pulse, and a slow bounded pulse-width envelope using the same melody and gate schedule. | Width stays in 12-bit range; an isolated note changes harmonic balance while the fundamental remains stable; full phrases retain clear melody/accompaniment separation. |
| Transient plus pitched body | Give one bass note a brief independently pitched transient, then restore the score pitch; test a pitched sweep and a noise transient separately. | The macro returns to the correct frequency before the bass body; no stale noise waveform remains; repeated bass attacks are audible without weakening the phrase. |
| Shared-filter ownership | Route accompaniment to one restrained filter envelope, leaving the melody direct; test single-trigger ownership against conflicting retriggers. | No simultaneous voice writes fight over cutoff/mode; section transitions have no abrupt filter reset; compare intended 8580 and a separately recorded 6581 rendering. |
| Harmony density | On a selected slow chord, compare one sustained inner note with a deliberately authored short arpeggio macro. | Retain original chord membership and bass/melody timings; the listener hears the harmony without a distracting tremolo; document the reduction choice. |

For these experiments, the core tool feature is an editable per-note sound program with separate musical timing and instrument timing. It needs bounded waveform/pitch/pulse/filter steps, explicit retrigger rules, and predictable shared-resource ownership. These proposals are inspired by the documented workflows; they are not claimed reconstructions of either composer's patch bytes.

The initial repository [three-voice builder](../../../scripts/build-arrangement.ts) configures one fixed waveform, envelope, and pulse width per voice and schedules frequency/gate writes. It proves the score plays, but that initial contract cannot express the instrument evolution examined here. Keep the normalized [common score](../../song-workflow.md) independent; richer native sound programs belong downstream or in direct SID authoring. Otherwise every new timbral decision would require altering source conversion.

My recommendation is to use an established native composing environment for instrument exploration while keeping reproducible conversion, builds, traces, and renders. [SID Factory II's maintainer](https://github.com/Chordian/sidfactory2) documents driver selection, tracker-style editing, imports, and both SID models; the [tool assessment](../tools/overview.md) also covers GoatTracker and SID-Wizard. A custom driver becomes worthwhile when a demonstrated musical need exceeds those tools. The historical lesson is that the composers' handmade systems exposed their musical ideas precisely, not that every new song should start by reinventing a tracker.
