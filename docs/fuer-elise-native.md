# Native Für Elise sound-design candidate

This candidate applies original SID-Wizard instrument programs to the complete, verified [three-voice Für Elise score](fuer-elise-score.md). The [native recipe](../songs/fuer-elise-sidwizard.json) chooses a coherent palette and section contrasts; the unchanged [baseline arrangement](../songs/fuer-elise.json) remains the source of all 981 selected note events. The recipe adds no source note events and edits neither the source pitches nor source JSON durations. Native quantization and the explicit short-note timing adjustment are recorded separately below. The finished native arrangement is not passed through MIDI again. Source preservation and native audio fidelity require separate converter/playback verification.

The user judged the original short [candidate mix](research/composition/instruments/README.md) “sounds ok,” but found this complete arrangement's opening high notes noise-like. The [triangle-attack variant](../songs/studies/fuer-elise-attack/README.md) was preferred for removing that quality, while remaining too simple or clinical. DRAX's reference was preferred for richness, playfulness, bass, crispness and perceived breadth. These bounded listening findings motivate a separate expressive interpretation; they do not establish a complete-song verdict. Playback equipment was unspecified. The [gesture library](research/composition/gestures/README.md) records the reusable musical direction.

The opening melody's actual register trace never selects noise. A controlled attack variant changes only `melody-pluck.wave[0][0]` from saw `$21` to triangle `$11`, retaining its following triangle stage. Over 1,004 calls, the only register differences are 72 melody attack-control values; pitch, envelope, timing, shared filter and other voices remain identical. Together with the listener's preference, this supports the triangle attack for this passage. It does not establish a universal preference for duller instruments: evaluate definition and motion in the intended register and mix. The canonical full-song recipe below retains its original attack; the alternative is a separately reproducible study.

## Musical form and palette

Section boundaries follow the baseline's source-verified performance timeline. Its main boundaries are 66½ for the F-major episode, 89 for the first thematic return, 120½ for the repeated-bass episode, 155 for the final return and 187½ for the written ending. The additional boundaries at 77 and 84½ bracket the thirty-second-note passage and its following dotted-eighth transition, and 147½ begins the triplet cadenza. These subdivisions are supported by the preserved [LilyPond source](../songs/sources/fuer-elise.ly) and the note starts/durations in [the normalized performance](../songs/sources/fuer-elise.score.json); they do not invent a new printed formal label.

| Performance quarter beats | Source passage | Melodic texture | Bass/support intention |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `[0, 66½)` | Main theme and both printed repeats | Brief saw attack into triangle body | Quiet filtered pulse bass and soft triangle accompaniment |
| `[66½, 77)` | F-major opening and sustained phrases | Moving pulse with delayed vibrato | Maintain restrained bass/support while the lead opens |
| `[77, 84½)` | Thirty-second-note runs | Bright pluck to keep the rapid line distinct | Keep the underlying voices restrained |
| `[84½, 89)` | Dotted-eighth transition | Moving pulse develops the held notes | Prepare the pluck's return |
| `[89, 120½)` | Returning theme | Restore the opening pluck | Restore the opening support balance |
| `[120½, 147½)` | Repeated-bass episode and held chords | Moving pulse over the longer chord-tone melody | Lower sustain and resonance for the dense bass pedal |
| `[147½, 155)` | Ascending/descending triplet cadenza | Bright pluck for the fast changing register | Keep each existing support note without adding a drum part |
| `[155, 187½)` | Final theme and written eighth rest | Triangle-only onset with a slightly longer release | Softer bass pedal patch for a changed, restrained final texture |

Instrument changes are selected by each existing note's onset; a boundary does not cut a held note in half. The grace notes before beat 66½ retain their quantized starts. Notes quantized to one or two PAL ticks use the explicit short-note instrument routing and retain an open gate through their body tick. A one-tick grace needs the timing adjustment described below to give the native player enough time to establish the correct audible pitch. No kick-to-bass instrument is placed in this first full-song candidate: the written repeated sixteenths already supply rhythmic motion, and a new accented per-note override would need its own listening reason. The independently editable kick patches remain available in [the bank](research/composition/instruments/README.md).

## Native instrument contract

Target: SID-Wizard 1.97 Extra, one MOS8580 at `$D400`, PAL, one player update per video frame. The instrument grammar retains separate waveform, pulse and filter programs, using the [native bank generator](../scripts/generate-native-instruments.py). The patches are original project variants of the implemented [bank](research/composition/instruments/README.md), with explicit changes for the complete arrangement's note density and balance. They are not historical recovered presets or copied third-party instruments.

- `ELPLUCK` derives the bright-attack/triangle-body design. After the standard TEST first frame, its waveform table selects saw `$21` for one update, then triangle `$11`; ADSR `$0754` raises the body sustain from the bank's 3 to 5 for the complete melody. The first audible stage therefore has the complete native frequency word rather than an inherited low byte.
- `ELPULSE` keeps the bank's pulse `$41`, width sweep `$400`–`$800` and delayed vibrato `$24`/12 after its TEST frame. ADSR `$0764` lowers sustain from the bank's 10 to 6. The waveform table ends after setting the body pitch, allowing continuous vibrato to develop on held notes. Short notes need not complete a full sweep or reach the vibrato delay.
- `ELSOFT` has a triangle waveform-table onset and body, ADSR `$0745`, with a softer attack spectrum and longer release for the final return. The release is a timbral tail rather than a source-duration extension.
- `ELGRACE` uses TEST then triangle, ADSR `$0352` and no pulse/filter program. It is the explicit short melodic-note variant; a minimum two-tick note lifetime is required so its body can sound at the complete native pitch.
- `ELBASS` keeps the plain pulse bass's width `$600` and low-pass/resonance-4 sweep 1024→640. ADSR `$0423` reduces the bank's sustain from 11 to 2 and speeds the decay, intended to place bass underneath the melody. Its pulse width and full pitch are established together at the first audible body update after TEST.
- `ELPEDAL` reduces sustain further to 1 with ADSR `$0312`, resonance 2 and a shorter 768→640 cutoff sweep. It is intended for the repetitive bass episode and the final return, where repeated attacks should support the phrase without becoming the foreground.
- `ELBSHORT` is an unfiltered TEST-then-triangle patch with ADSR `$0322` for a source bass note that quantizes to one or two ticks. It avoids relying on pulse/filter initialization during its short audible body.
- `ELHARM` uses TEST then triangle, ADSR `$0422` and no modulation/filter program, so accompaniment remains distinct from the moving pulse melody and does not compete for shared filter control.

All patches deliberately retain `frame1=$09`: the standard TEST interval keeps the incomplete first-frame frequency inaudible, then each patch's distinct waveform program establishes its audible attack/body one PAL frame after the quantized note onset, approximately 19.95 ms later. This common first-frame choice follows the reproduced pitch constraint below; it does not make the waveform, pulse, filter or vibrato programs equivalent. The normal bass and pedal patches alone control the shared filter; the melody and accompaniment do not. No instrument uses ring modulation or oscillator sync. ADSR sustain values are balance choices, not calibrated per-voice gain in decibels, and the attack peaks still need mix-level listening review. The other SID model and physical hardware require separate evidence.

## Timing and verification boundary

The [native bridge](../scripts/arrange-sidwizard.py) encodes one row per PAL tick, splits each voice into editable patterns, and compresses runs of empty rows. The native limits remain 100 patterns, at most 248 rows per pattern, and 249 unpacked bytes per pattern including its end marker. Instrument/effect columns consume part of that unpacked byte budget. A compressed NOP represents two to nine empty rows; the SWM footer retains the original unpacked byte count because SID-Maker uses it to locate preceding pattern and sequence data during backwards parsing. The bridge therefore splits by the unpacked budget before compression and deduplication. Its conversion report distinguishes `packedBytes` and `unpackedBytes`; both include the end-marker position and exclude the extra saved row-length byte. The [format guide](research/tools/sid-wizard-trial.md) explains the parsing rule and regression checks.

The source preserves the complete form, original provenance and reduction report from [the baseline score](fuer-elise-score.md), including the two restored repeats, grace-note positions, all three selected voices, the high triplet cadenza and the final written rest. Native conversion must independently account for all 554 melody, 181 bass and 246 harmony events without collision or deletion. Quantization and native articulation must be reported separately from preservation of the input JSON.

At one-tick row tempo, the stock Extra player's initial-frame path writes the new frequency high byte while retaining the previous low byte; the waveform table establishes the complete pitch on the following continuous update. `frame1=$09` keeps that incomplete first frame silent. Immediate `$11` or `$21` can sound an incorrect pitch: an E4 target of 5613 can initially use 5376, approximately 75 cents flat. Therefore a nonzero waveform and decoded note index are insufficient to verify a very short note. The basis is `STRTSND`/`SETWFR1` and `FASTSPEEDBIND` in [the pinned player source](../.tools/sid-wizard-1.97/native/sources/include/player.asm).

The `$3F` legato route also defers the full frequency update until a continuous-update frame. An intervening one-frame note can be skipped entirely; legato is not a workaround for this constraint. Use a minimum two-frame lifetime and verify gate, pitched waveform, TEST clear and full frequency on the second frame. [The native workflow](native-song-workflow.md) enforces that check for critical short notes.

The recipe explicitly selects `timingPolicy: "retain-full-pitch"`. This stock-player policy preserves the complete audible grace pitch by extending a note that quantizes to one tick to two ticks. It uses one tick of following silence when available, or borrows one tick from a contiguous following note when that note has room to retain an audible body. A borrowed note's endpoint and the phrase's endpoint remain unchanged. Every adjustment must be explicit in the conversion report; lack of sufficient following space or borrowing budget must fail rather than delete the grace or hide a wrong pitch. For this source, the affected melody event is index 206, B-flat4/MIDI 70, at beat 71.9713541667: its quantized interval `[3006,3007)` becomes `[3006,3008)`, and the following A4/MIDI 69 moves its onset from tick 3007 to 3008 while preserving its endpoint. This intentionally trades approximately 19.95 ms of local rhythmic precision for a complete native pitched grace; it is not an unchanged-duration claim. The two-tick F4 grace at melody index 189 retains its interval and must keep the gate open on its second/body tick. The independent oracle must verify waveform, gate and complete frequency word on actual grace body ticks 2774 and 3007, not only their decoded pitch indices. Source, quantized and adjusted native timing remain separate report fields.

Consecutive notes retain the native driver's envelope behaviour, including its lack of a separate gate restart at one-tick row tempo; the TEST interval separates their audible oscillator stages. No driver modification is used. Keep rendered, native C64 and listening checks separate from source-note accounting.

## Native ending

End each voice's final pattern with a plain, single-column `$7E` gate-off. Put the persistent volume-zero command `$A0` followed by stop `$FE` in **each** orderlist. A compound gate-off/effect cell is unsafe at the ending: the stop path can leave the pattern cursor on an effect operand and later interpret it as a note. With the declared ending on all three voices, gates and master volume are clear on the stop call itself. [Native export tests](../tests/sidwizard_export_test.py) exercise this rule through the original player, while the full-song oracle rejects any pitched restart after stop.

Once rendered, retain the native SWM/SWI files, SID and standalone PRG, timing/conversion report, exact write log, original-amplitude WAV, fixed review excerpts and any level-matched comparisons in ignored `build/`. Record the editor/exporter identity, model, cadence, source and output checksums, source-to-native event mapping, filter ownership, CPU measurements, release ending and the user's complete-song verdict. The [source workflow](song-workflow.md) and [native exporter trial](research/tools/sid-wizard-trial.md) remain the reproduction basis.
